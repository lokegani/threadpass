How a ThreadPass finding is produced

Written out in full, because a compliance record nobody can audit is worth very little to the buyer reading it.

Two layers, deliberately independent

Layer 1 — satellite. A published water-quality index, NDTI, is computed from free Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery over the river stretch that receives this unit's discharge. It covers the whole stretch continuously and cannot be evaded by timing a discharge around an inspection visit, because there is no visit to time it around.

Layer 2 — ground sensor. A low-cost node at the unit's own effluent outlet logs pH, total dissolved solids and turbidity. This supplies the one thing satellite imagery cannot: a reading attributable to this specific unit rather than to the river in general.

The layers are useful precisely because they fail differently. Cloud cover blinds the satellite but not the sensor. A sensor that is unplugged, fouled or bypassed says nothing about the river the satellite is still watching.

Each channel is scored against its own baseline

There is no fixed threshold that applies to every unit. A mercerising line and a bleaching-and-scouring line have genuinely different normal ranges, and a single national limit would flag the wrong one. Every channel is therefore scored against its own 30-day median.

Spread is measured with MAD — the median absolute deviation — rescaled by 1.4826 so it estimates the same quantity a standard deviation would for well-behaved data.

Why not a standard deviation? Because the thing we are trying to detect would corrupt it. A four-day discharge episode inflates the standard deviation of the very series it appears in, widening the "normal" band until the episode sits comfortably inside it. A median of absolute deviations does not move like that: a handful of extreme values cannot drag it, which is the entire reason it is used here.

Only sustained departures count

A reading must sit at least robust standard deviations from the unit's own median, and must stay there for or more consecutive days, before it is recorded as an excursion at all.

A single odd day is a cloud edge, a passing boat, a sensor settling after maintenance. Two consecutive days is a pattern. This rule discards most of what would otherwise be noise, at the cost of missing a genuine single-day event — a trade made deliberately, in favour of not crying wolf.

The rule that decides everything

What was observedWhat ThreadPass recordsEffect on eligibility
A satellite excursion and a sensor excursion overlapping in the same window Flagged A verified violation. Certification is withheld or revoked.
A satellite excursion with nothing on the ground Watch None. Disclosed in the report, does not affect eligibility.
A sensor excursion with nothing overhead Watch None. Disclosed in the report, does not affect eligibility.
Fewer than 30 days of paired history Not Yet Eligible No verdict is offered. Absence of findings is not a clean result.

Requiring both layers to agree is what makes a Flagged event worth acting on. It is also what protects a compliant unit from being penalised for a discharge that was never theirs.

What this system does not claim

It is cluster and outlet-level monitoring, not pipe-level forensic proof. It will not establish which of two adjacent units released a given litre of effluent, and it is not designed to.

It is a self-monitoring and disclosure record the unit holds and chooses to show a buyer — not an enforcement instrument, and not a regulatory or statutory certification. It confers no legal compliance status.

It is a water-quality and compliance tool, not a climate technology, and is not presented as one. The genuine indirect connection is that enforcement of existing Zero Liquid Discharge and effluent-reuse mandates depends on reliable monitoring, which this supports rather than replaces.

And in this build specifically: every reading is simulated. No satellite imagery is fetched and no sensor exists. The unit names and registration numbers are fictional. What is real here is the method — the baseline, the MAD threshold, the sustained-run rule and the dual-layer correlation all run exactly as described above on the generated data.
